## The generic-entry trap
Most entries will be: RAG chatbot over government documents, thin civic wrapper, generic dashboard. Judges will have seen 50 by lunch. **Do not be one of them.** Our edge must be visible in the first 10 seconds of the demo.

## Submission mechanics (the hidden rules)
- **Updatable submissions** → upload v1 on D7 (18 Sep), refine until the end. Early = derisk, and it forces the full assembly once.
- **Five winners** → target top-5 consistency across all four criteria, not perfection in one.
- **Cross-track welcome** → we can straddle if genuinely both; don't force it.
- **"Refrain from using AI to generate your capstone idea"** → the idea must trace to our own thinking (it does: SPSS, first thoughts doc dated). Keep that trail visible.

## Vocabulary alignment (use truthfully, sparingly)
OSF's Africa programs language: locally-led, community-rooted, shift power to communities, ubuntu (dignity/justice/solidarity), African innovation and leadership. Mentions should map to real features, never decoration.

## The 10-second test
Can a judge answer — after 10 seconds of the video — *what makes this different from a chatbot?* If not, rebuild the opening.

## Insurance
- Everything important exists in writing in the repo (judges may read more than they watch).
- Summary written for a non-technical reviewer; deck readable on a phone.
- No claim without a demoed feature or a cited source.
